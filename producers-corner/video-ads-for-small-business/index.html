Video ads for small business: a producer's guide.

By Zach Wennstedt, founder of Madwackd Studio · Updated November 6, 2026

Short answer: a small-business video ad works best when it's short, hooks people in the first three seconds, makes one offer, and tells viewers exactly what to do next. TikTok's own guide recommends 21 to 34 seconds for most ads. You can make one with AI for far less than a film crew, then test a few versions before spending real money.

Most "video ads for small business" guides are template galleries. Templates are fine, but they skip the part that decides whether the ad pays off: what you say, in what order, and where the click goes. This guide is the producer's version, built on what the ad platforms themselves publish.

Why video, and why now?

Video is now the default, not the extra. Wyzowl's 2026 survey found 91% of businesses use video in their marketing, and 41% of marketers paid for video ads this year, up from 36%. 63% of video marketers say they've used AI to make video, up from 51% a year earlier. (Wyzowl, 2026; Wyzowl is a video company, 266 respondents.)

Small businesses are adopting AI fast too, though the numbers depend on who's asking and how. The U.S. Census Bureau found 19.8% of U.S. businesses used AI in some business function as of May 3, 2026 (Census Bureau, May 2026). The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's 2026 small-business report says two-thirds of small businesses now use AI (U.S. Chamber, Oct 2026). Different surveys, different definitions, same direction.

How long should a small-business video ad be?

For TikTok, the platform says it "recommends 21-34 seconds for most ads" (TikTok for Business video advertising guide). Marketers in Wyzowl's survey lean the same way: 71% say videos between 30 seconds and 2 minutes work best. Our advice: make a 20 to 30 second main cut, plus a 6 to 10 second version of just the hook and offer for retargeting.

How to make a video ad for your small business, step by step

1. Pick one customer and one action

"Everyone" isn't a target. Write one sentence: "Homeowners in our area who need X should call us this week because Y." That sentence is your ad.

2. Hook them in the first three seconds

TikTok's guide puts it plainly: "Hook viewers in the first three seconds," and skip long intros and logo animations. Open on the problem, the result, or something surprising, not your logo.

3. Show, then say

Show the product, the before-and-after, or the moment of relief. Put captions on the screen, because plenty of people scroll with the sound off.

4. Make one offer and one call to action

"Book this week, get X" beats a list of everything you do. End with the exact action: call, book, or visit, and show it on screen.

5. Make three versions, not one

Change only the hook between versions. Run them side by side with a small daily budget, and move the money to the one that brings in leads, not just views.

6. Label AI content honestly

Ad platforms ask you to disclose realistic AI-generated content. On TikTok that's a setting in Ads Manager. Turn it on when it applies. Honest labels don't hurt a good ad, and hiding them can get one pulled.

7. Send the click somewhere that converts

A great ad that lands on a slow, confusing page wastes the budget. Make sure the page matches the ad's offer and loads fast on a phone. If you don't have a site that does that yet, Eye To Ad, part of the Madwackd family, builds websites made to get local businesses found.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Logo first. Nobody stays for a logo animation. Put it at the end.
  • Too many messages. One offer per ad. Make another ad for the next offer.
  • Fake proof. Invented reviews and testimonials can break FTC rules. Use real customers, with their permission, or skip it.
  • Judging too early. Give each version enough spend and days to collect real results before picking a winner.
  • Counting views instead of customers. Track calls, bookings and form fills.

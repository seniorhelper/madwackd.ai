How much does an AI voiceover cost? AI vs a voice actor.

By Zach Wennstedt, founder of Madwackd Studio · Updated November 8, 2026

Short answer: an AI voiceover on a voice platform costs from $0 (free tiers, usually without commercial rights) to about $22 a month for creator plans with over 100 minutes of audio, prices checked November 8, 2026. A human voice actor runs about $100 to $500 for a 15 to 60 second commercial on a 2026 marketplace guide, and union TV work starts at a session fee of about $502 plus usage. In Madwackd Studio the voice is part of the video: an 8-second shot with voice is about 24 credits, $2.40 to $3.40, and the exact quote shows before you render.

"How much does an AI voiceover cost" has three honest answers, because there are three different things you can buy: minutes of audio from a voice platform, a human performance priced by usage, or a finished video with the voice already in it. This guide prices all three with sources and dates, then shows when the human is worth the money.

AI voice platform pricing (dated)

Platforms sell audio in credits that roughly equal minutes. As the most-searched example, ElevenLabs listed these on its pricing page on November 8, 2026: Free $0 for 10,000 credits (about 10 minutes, no commercial license listed); Starter $6 for 30,000; Creator $22 for 121,000; Pro $99 for 600,000; Scale $299 for 1.8 million; Business $990 for 6 million, with a commercial license from Starter up. Other platforms are in the same shape: a free tier without commercial rights, a creator tier in the tens of dollars, pro tiers around a hundred. Two costs hide behind the plan price: regenerations count against your minutes, and the video is a separate tool and a separate bill.

Human voice actor rates

Human rates are set by usage, not by the minute: the same 30-second read costs more on national TV than on your own website. Numbers you can check:

  • A marketplace pricing guide dated March 31, 2026 lists commercials (15 to 60 seconds) at $100 to $500 per project, explainer and corporate narration at $150 to $800, e-learning at $0.10 to $0.50 a word or $20 to $100 per finished minute, and union talent at $2,000 and up per project (Twine, 2026; a freelance marketplace, unattributed ranges).
  • A working union voice actor's rate page lists SAG-AFTRA baselines of about $502 session fee plus usage for TV commercials and about $282 plus usage for radio (D.C. Douglas; undated, "general guides").
  • The non-union reference most actors quote is the GVAA Rate Guide, which is behind a sign-up and not reproduced here. Expect usage tiers: web-only, local, regional, national.

Budget realistically: a good non-union read for a web ad is a few hundred dollars; a national TV campaign with a known voice is thousands plus residuals. Traditional production costs around the voice are in our guide to how much a commercial costs.

When a human voice is worth it

  • The voice is the brand. A recurring spokesperson, a sound people recognize.
  • Real emotion carries the ad. Grief, comedy timing, a laugh that lands.
  • Broadcast and big media. Usage terms, union rules and station standards are built for human talent.
  • You need someone to direct. A good actor fixes a weak script in the booth.

For most short social ads, explainers and tests, an AI voice in the right style does the job, and the money is better spent on more versions and more testing.

Hidden costs to budget for either way

  • Commercial license. Free voices usually are not free for ads.
  • Regenerations and retakes. Both AI minutes and human sessions get used up by changes. Lock the script first.
  • Music and sound. Licensed music is its own line item.
  • Editing time. Yours or someone's. A built-in studio removes the line-up step.
  • Consent paperwork. Any custom or cloned voice needs written permission; see our consent guide.

Questions people ask

Is an AI voiceover cheaper than a voice actor?

Per minute of audio, yes, by a wide margin: creator plans on voice platforms run from free to about $22 a month for over 100 minutes, while a 2026 marketplace guide puts a human-read 15 to 60 second commercial at $100 to $500 and a union TV session fee at about $502 plus usage. The gap narrows once you count the video, editing and licensing you still need around the voice.

How much does an AI voiceover cost per minute?

On ElevenLabs, checked November 8, 2026, the Creator plan is $22 a month for about 121 minutes, roughly 18 cents a minute if you use it all; the Free plan gives about 10 minutes with no commercial license. Madwackd Studio does not sell voice by the minute: the voice is part of an 8-second video shot that costs about 24 credits, $2.40 to $3.40, with the quote shown before you render.

Are free AI voices free for commercial use?

Usually not. Free tiers on voice platforms commonly exclude commercial use, which includes ads and monetized channels. Read the plan page and keep a copy of the terms with the project.

What does a 30-second AI voiceover cost?

On a voice platform, 30 seconds is a fraction of any paid plan, so the real cost is the monthly plan and the video you still have to make. In Madwackd Studio a 30-second clip with voice is about four 8-second Fast 720p shots, roughly 96 credits or $9.60 to $13.44 before retakes, quoted before you confirm. A producer-made 30-second spot starts at $2,500.

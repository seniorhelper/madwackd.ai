How to add an AI voiceover to a video: a producer's guide.

By Zach Wennstedt, founder of Madwackd Studio · Updated November 8, 2026

Short answer: write the script for the ear, pick a voice style that matches the job, lock the picture before you generate the final voice, fix pronunciation in the script, place the voice under the video and duck the music, check the license and the AI label, then export per platform. You can do it with a separate text-to-speech tool and an editor, or in one studio that puts the voice on the video and shows you the price before it renders.

Most "add an AI voiceover" guides are three clicks inside one app. Those clicks are the easy part. What decides whether the voiceover sounds like a person or a GPS is the script, the timing and the mix, and those are the same whatever tool you use. This is how our producers do it, in the order we do it.

How to add an AI voiceover to a video, step by step

1. Write for the ear, not the page

Short sentences. One idea each. Contractions. Read it out loud with a timer: a normal narration pace is roughly 150 words a minute, so a 25-second ad holds about 60 words and an 8-second shot about 20. If you run out of breath, the voice will sound rushed too. The free script fitter in our Creator Studio splits your script into 8-second shots and shows the credit estimate before you spend anything.

2. Pick the voice style for the job

Match the voice to the promise. A documentary read sells trust; a trailer read sells scale; a friendly-neighbor read sells a local service; an upbeat promo read sells a deal. Madwackd's stock styles are exactly those five: narrator, trailer, upbeat promo, friendly neighbor and documentary. Generate a test line in two styles and play them to someone who did not write the script.

3. Lock the picture first

Change a shot after the voice is done and every line lands late. Finish the cut, or in a studio workflow finish the shot list, then generate the final voice. Rough voice for timing is fine; final voice comes last.

4. Generate, listen, fix the script

Most "robotic" problems live in the script, not the voice. Spell numbers the way you say them ("twenty twenty-six"), break acronyms with spaces or periods, and respell brand names phonetically if the voice stumbles. Add a comma where you want a breath. Regenerate only the line you changed.

5. Place it and mix it

Voice on its own track, music underneath and ducked so the words stay clear, sound effects where the picture needs them. Most people scroll with the sound off, so add captions as well; the caption maker exports SRT and VTT for free.

6. Check the license and the label

Free tiers of voice tools often exclude commercial use, which includes ads and monetized channels. Read the plan before you publish. Ad platforms also ask you to label realistic AI-generated content; some have a setting for it. Madwackd puts an invisible provenance mark on every clip, and any custom voice goes through a consent check first.

7. Export for each place it will play

9:16 for Reels, Shorts and TikTok, 16:9 for YouTube and your site. Keep faces and text out of the areas the apps cover; the free safe-zone checker shows where those are.

Text to speech for YouTube videos: what's different

Longer scripts, so pace matters more. Break a 10-minute script into sections and generate each one, so a late change does not mean regenerating everything. Use one voice for the whole channel so returning viewers recognize it. Faceless channels live or die on the script; a good voice cannot save a flat one. YouTube allows synthetic narration, and its policies on repetitive content still apply, so check the current rules before you build a channel on it.

Mistakes we see most

  • Generating the final voice before the cut is locked. Every change after that costs a regeneration.
  • One long paragraph. Voices breathe at punctuation. Give them some.
  • Using a free voice in a paid ad. Check the license.
  • Music louder than the words. Duck it.
  • No captions. Most feeds start muted.

Questions people ask

Can ChatGPT do voiceovers?

Chat assistants can talk to you, but a video voiceover needs an audio file you can place on a timeline, or a studio that puts the voice on the video for you. Use a text-to-speech tool that exports audio, or a video studio with voice built in. Check the tool's license before using the result in anything you sell or advertise.

How do I add a voiceover onto a video?

Write the script for the ear, lock your picture edit, generate the voice, listen and fix pronunciation, then place the audio under the video and duck the music beneath it. In Madwackd Studio the voice is added inside the same project as the shot, and the credit price shows before anything renders.

How can I add an AI voiceover to a video for free?

Several tools have free tiers, and most of them do not include a commercial license, so a free voice is fine for a personal clip and not for an ad. Madwackd's free Creator Studio tools help you write hooks, fit your script to 8-second shots and make captions with no account; the render itself needs credits, from a $5 Test Drive.

How long should a voiceover script be?

Plan on roughly 150 spoken words a minute at a normal narration pace, so a 25-second ad holds about 60 words and an 8-second shot holds about 20. Read it out loud with a timer; a script that reads well on paper is often too long when spoken.

Do I have to say a video uses an AI voice?

Ad platforms ask you to label realistic AI-generated content, and some have a setting for it in their ads tools. Madwackd Studio puts an invisible provenance mark on every clip. Check the current rules of the platform you publish on.
